# Art. 926 codice civile - Migrazione di colombi, conigli e pesci

## Summary
Art. 926 of the Italian Civil Code (part of Book III, Title II, Chapter III, Section I – Occupation and Invention) governs the acquisition of ownership of animals that pass from one enclosure to another. It establishes that rabbits and fish moving to a different rabbitry or fishpond are acquired by the owner of that enclosure, provided they were not attracted there by artifice or fraud. The same rule applies to pigeons moving to another dovecote, subject to special laws on homing pigeons.

## Key Details
- Animals covered: rabbits, fish, and pigeons (colombi).
- Acquisition occurs automatically upon passage to another conigliera (rabbitry) or peschiera (fishpond).
- Exception: if the animals were attracted with *arte o frode* (artifice or fraud), the original owner retains ownership.
- Identical rule for pigeons moving to a different colombaia (dovecote).
- Carrier pigeons (colombi viaggiatori) are subject to separate legislation (e.g., law on racing pigeons).
- Article is located in the section on modes of acquiring property (occupation and invention).
