# Art. 983 codice civile - Accessioni

## Summary
Art. 983 of the Italian Civil Code establishes that the right of usufruct extends to all accessions (natural or artificial additions) to the thing subject to usufruct. It also regulates the situation where the owner, with the usufructuary's consent or by order of public authority, makes constructions or plantations after the usufruct has begun: in that case, the usufructuary must pay interest on the sums invested.

## Key Details
- The usufruct covers all accessions of the thing, including natural increments and man-made additions (reference to [[art817]], [[art934]] and [[art1998]]).
- If the owner, after the start of the usufruct and with the usufructuary's consent, builds or plants on the property, the usufructuary must pay interest (at the legal rate, see [[art1284]]) on the capital employed.
- The same rule applies when constructions or plantations are made by order of the public authority (e.g., compulsory works).
- The provision aims to balance the interests of the owner (who improves the property) and the usufructuary (who enjoys the fruit but does not bear the cost of capital).
- Related to other articles on usufruct such as [[art1005]] (obligations of the usufructuary for ordinary repairs) and [[art1009]] (obligations for extraordinary repairs).
