# Art. 823 codice civile – Condizione giuridica del demanio pubblico

## Summary
Art. 823 of the Italian Civil Code defines the legal status of assets forming part of the public domain (demanio pubblico). It establishes their absolute inalienability and the impossibility of third parties acquiring rights over them, except in the cases and within the limits expressly provided by law. The article also entrusts the administrative authority with the protection of such assets, allowing it to proceed either through administrative channels or by availing itself of the ordinary remedies for the protection of property and possession regulated by the Civil Code.

## Key Details
- Assets belonging to the public domain are **inalienable**.
- They cannot be the object of rights in favor of third parties, except as permitted by specific laws (e.g., concessions, expropriations).
- The administrative authority (e.g., State, regions, municipalities) is responsible for the protection of public domain assets.
- Protection may be exercised **in via amministrativa** (administrative action) or through **ordinary judicial remedies** for property and possession (actions under the Civil Code).
- This provision reinforces the principle of public interest overriding private interests with regard to demanial goods.
