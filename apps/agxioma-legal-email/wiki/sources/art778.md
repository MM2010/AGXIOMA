# Art. 778 codice civile - Mandato a donare

## Summary
Art. 778 c.c. establishes the general rule that a mandate to donate is null when it entrusts the agent with the power to designate the donee or to determine the object of the donation [[art1703]], [[art1418]]. However, the article provides two exceptions: the donation is valid if a third party chooses the beneficiary among a class or category pre‑set by the donor, or if a third party determines the object among specified things or within a value limit fixed by the donor (mirroring the rule for testamentary dispositions under [[art631]]). This norm balances the need for a definite gift with the flexibility to delegate certain choices.

## Key Details
- The mandate to donate is **null** if it confers on the agent the power to **choose the donee** or to **determine the object** of the donation.
- The nullity follows the general regime of contract nullity ([[art1418]] and following).
- **Exception 1**: A donation is valid when a third party selects the beneficiary **from a list of persons designated by the donor** or **from a specific category** (e.g., “the neediest students of University X”).
- **Exception 2**: A donation is valid when the donee is a **legal entity** among those indicated by the donor (e.g., “one of the following charities”).
- **Exception 3**: The donation is valid if a third party determines the object **among several things indicated by the donor** or **within a value limit set by the donor**.
- The exceptions align with the rules for testamentary bequests under [[art631 comma 2 c.c.]].
- The article safeguards the **donor’s intent** while allowing a degree of delegation, preventing the uncertainty that would arise from an entirely open power.
