# Art. 111 codice penale - Determinazione al reato di persona non imputabile o non punibile

## Summary
Art. 111 cp establishes that anyone who induces a person who is not imputable (e.g., minors under 14, persons of unsound mind) or not punishable due to a personal condition or quality (e.g., diplomatic immunity, error induced by deception) to commit a crime is criminally liable for that crime. The penalty is increased; further aggravations apply if the induced person is the inducer's child under parental responsibility, and when the crime is one for which flagrante arrest is permitted. This provision extends criminal responsibility to the indirect perpetrator who uses a non‑accountable agent as an instrument.

## Key Details
- **Subject**: The inducer (*determinatore*) who causes a non‑imputable or non‑punible person to commit a crime.
- **Non‑imputable persons** are defined by artt. 86, 88, 96, 97, 98 cp (e.g., total mental incapacity, alcohol/drug intoxication, age <14).
- **Non‑punible persons due to personal condition** refer to situations like absolute immunity (art. 46 cp – coercion) or error induced by deception (art. 48 cp).
- The inducer is liable for the crime actually committed by the induced person, with the ordinary penalty increased.
- **First aggravating circumstance**: if the crime is one for which arrest in flagrante is provided (delitti per i quali è previsto l'arresto in flagranza), the increase is from one‑third to one‑half.
- **Second aggravating circumstance**: if the inducer is the parent exercising parental responsibility, the increase is up to one‑half (or, for flagrante‑arrest delitti, from one‑third to two‑thirds).
- This rule is part of the broader *concorso di persone nel reato* (Title IV, Capo III, artt. 110–119 cp) and complements the rules on indirect perpetration.
