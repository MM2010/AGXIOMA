# Art. 562 codice penale - Pena accessoria e sanzione civile

## Summary
Art. 562 c.p., part of the Italian Penal Code (Title XI – Crimes against the Family, Chapter I – Crimes against Marriage), originally provided that conviction for certain matrimonial crimes (articles 556 and 560) entailed the loss of *autorità maritale* (marital authority). Additionally, in cases of adultery or concubinage, the judge could order temporary civil measures for the protection of the offended spouse and children, upon request. These measures were immediately enforceable but lapsed if a petition for personal separation was not filed before the civil court within three months of the conviction becoming final. The article was declared unconstitutional by the Constitutional Court with judgment no. 147 of 3 December 1969.

## Key Details
- **Article number**: 562 of the Italian Penal Code.
- **Context**: Located in Book II, Title XI (Crimes against the Family), Chapter I (Crimes against Marriage).
- **Original content**: 
  - First paragraph: conviction for crimes under [[art556]] or [[art560]] entailed loss of *autorità maritale* (marital authority).
  - Second paragraph: upon conviction for adultery or concubinage, the judge could order urgent temporary civil measures on request of the offended spouse.
  - Third paragraph: such measures were immediately executory but ceased to have effect if a petition for personal separation ([[Separazione personale]]) was not filed before the civil court within three months of the final conviction.
- **Constitutional status**: Declared illegitimate by the Italian Constitutional Court (sent. 3 December 1969, n. 147). Therefore no longer in force.
- **Historical significance**: Reflected a now-obsolete legal framework where marital authority was a punitive consequence of certain matrimonial offenses; the ruling reflects evolving constitutional principles on equality and family protection.
