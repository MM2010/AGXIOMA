# Art. 814 codice civile - Energie

## Summary
Article 814 of the Italian Civil Code establishes that natural energies possessing economic value are considered movable property (beni mobili). This provision extends the concept of "goods" beyond tangible objects to include exploitable energy sources (e.g., electricity, solar, wind, geothermal), thereby subjecting them to the same legal regime applicable to movable assets, including rules on ownership, transfer, and protection.

## Key Details
- Located in Book III (Property), Title I (Goods), Chapter I (Goods in general), Section II (Immovable and movable goods).
- The article explicitly equates natural energies with economic value to movable goods.
- It does not apply to human or animal energy, nor to energies lacking economic value.
- The classification allows for legal transactions (sale, lease, hypothecation) over energy as a good.
- It underpins modern energy law, enabling contractual frameworks for electricity supply, renewable energy credits, etc.
- Previously, Italian law followed the Roman principle *res incorporales*; this codification made energy legally objectifiable.
- The article is short but foundational: it enables the entire energy market legal structure.
- Distinguished from [[art812]] (immovables) and [[art813]] (movables by legal determination).
