# Art. 41 codice penale - Concorso di cause

## Summary
Art. 41 of the Italian Penal Code governs the concurrence of causes in criminal causation. It establishes that pre‑existing, simultaneous, or supervening causes—even if independent of the offender’s conduct—do not break the causal link between the act or omission and the event, unless the supervening cause alone was sufficient to produce the event. If the preceding act or omission itself constitutes a crime, the penalty for that crime applies. The same rules apply when the cause is another person’s unlawful act.

## Key Details
- The article confirms that multiple causes (pre‑existing, simultaneous, or supervening) do not automatically exclude causality.
- A supervening cause **eliminates** the causal link only if it was **alone sufficient** to determine the event.
- If the original act or omission is itself an independent crime, the offender is punished for that crime (even if the causal link to the final event is broken).
- The provision applies also when the intervening cause is the **unlawful act of a third party**.
- This rule is a cornerstone of the objective imputation of criminal liability in Italian law.
- It is closely related to the theory of causality and the principle of _condicio sine qua non_ (Art. 40 CP).
