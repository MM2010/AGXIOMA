# Art. 200 codice penale - Applicabilità delle misure di sicurezza rispetto al tempo, al territorio e alle persone

## Summary
Art. 200 of the Italian Penal Code governs the temporal, territorial, and personal applicability of security measures (*misure di sicurezza*). Unlike criminal penalties, which are subject to the principle of non‑retroactivity (*nullum crimen, nulla poena sine lege*), security measures are regulated by the law in force at the time of their **application** (not at the time of the offense). If the law changes between the order of application and the actual execution, the law in force at the time of execution prevails. The article also extends the application of security measures to foreigners present in the Italian territory, while preserving the possibility of their expulsion under public security laws.

## Key Details
- Temporal rule: Security measures are governed by the law in force at the moment of their application, **not** at the time the offense was committed.
- If the law in force at the time of execution differs from the law at the time of application, the executing law applies (dynamic retroactivity).
- This rule is an exception to the strict non‑retroactivity principle that governs penal sanctions, reflecting the preventive (non‑punitive) character of security measures.
- Territorial scope: Security measures apply to any person, including foreigners, who is present in the Italian territory.
- However, application of a security measure to a foreigner does not prevent his/her expulsion under public security legislation (e.g., combined with [[art4-cp]] on territoriality).
- The provision is part of the general rules on personal security measures (Chapter I, Title VIII, Book I of the Penal Code).
