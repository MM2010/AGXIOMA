# Art. 2046 Codice Civile - Imputabilità del fatto dannoso

## Summary
Art. 2046 of the Italian Civil Code establishes that a person is not liable for the consequences of a harmful act if, at the time of committing it, they lacked the capacity to understand or will (*capacità d'intendere o di volere*). However, this exclusion does not apply if the state of incapacity was caused by the person's own fault (e.g., voluntary intoxication). The article sets the baseline rule for subjective imputability in tort law, distinguishing between natural incapacity and self-induced incapacity.

## Key Details
- Located in Book IV "Delle obbligazioni", Title IX "Dei fatti illeciti".
- The key condition: lack of *capacità d'intendere o di volere* at the exact moment of the harmful act.
- The defense of incapacity is unavailable if the incapacity itself derives from the person's own fault (*colpa*). This mirrors the principle of *actio libera in causa* (see [[art87-cp]]).
- Cross-referenced with [[art2047]] (liability of parents/guardians for damages caused by incapable persons) and [[art87-cp]] (penal code provision on self-induced incapacity).
- The assessment of capacity is strictly temporal: the moment the act is committed.
- The burden of proving the lack of capacity lies with the person invoking the exemption (according to general principles of evidence).
- This article applies to both contractual and extra‑contractual liability under the civil code.
