# Art. 93 Codice Penale - Fatto commesso sotto l'azione di sostanze stupefacenti

## Summary
Art. 93 of the Italian Penal Code extends the provisions of [[art91-cp]] and [[art92-cp]] (relating to intoxication) to acts committed under the influence of narcotic or psychotropic substances. The article thereby treats drug‑induced incapacity analogously to alcoholic intoxication, applying the same criteria of imputability when the agent has voluntarily assumed the substance or when the state of intoxication was not accidental. This provision is central to determining criminal responsibility in cases of drug‑related offenses.

## Key Details
- The article explicitly states: *“Le disposizioni dei due articoli precedenti si applicano anche quando il fatto è stato commesso sotto l'azione di sostanze stupefacenti.”*
- It directly incorporates the rules of Art. 91 and 92 into the context of narcotic substances.
- Art. 91 excludes imputability for accidental intoxication (fortuitous or force majeure) but only if the person had no intent to consume alcohol/drugs.
- Art. 92 reduces or excludes imputability for pre‑ordained intoxication (purposeful consumption to commit a crime or to prepare an excuse), and for habitual intoxication.
- Therefore, under Art. 93, the same logic applies: unintentional drug use may lead to non‑imputability; voluntary or premeditated use does not excuse the crime.
- The article does not define “sostanze stupefacenti” – that is left to special laws (e.g., D.P.R. 309/1990).
- A footnote in the source refers to an article 729 of the Penal Code (likely a cross‑reference to the repeal or transition rules for narcotic‑related norms).
- The rule is part of Title IV (*Del reo e della persona offesa dal reato*) – Chapter I (*Della imputabilità*), hence directly linked to the general theory of capacity to commit a crime.
