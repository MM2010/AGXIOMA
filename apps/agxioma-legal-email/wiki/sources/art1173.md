# Art. 1173 Codice Civile – Fonti delle obbligazioni

## Summary
This article is the foundational provision of Book IV of the Italian Civil Code, establishing the three categories of sources from which obligations arise: contract (*contratto*), tort (*fatto illecito*), and any other act or fact that, under the legal system, is capable of producing them. It marks the starting point for the entire law of obligations, delineating the boundaries of contractual liability, extra‑contractual liability, and other legally relevant events (e.g., unilateral promises, negotiorum gestio, undue payment).

## Key Details
- Placed in Book IV (*Delle obbligazioni*), Title I, Chapter I – preliminary provisions.
- First source: **Contract** – obligations voluntarily assumed by agreement (see [[art1321]]).
- Second source: **Tort** – obligations arising from unlawful acts causing damage (see [[art2043]]).
- Third source: **Any other act or fact** capable of producing obligations “in conformity with the legal order” – a residual clause covering e.g., unilateral promises ([[art1987]]), negotiorum gestio ([[art2028]]), undue payment ([[art2033]]), and family‑law obligations.
- The expression “idoneo a produrle in conformità dell’ordinamento giuridico” ensures flexibility: only acts or facts that the legal system recognises as obligation‑generating are included.
- This article does **not** provide an exhaustive list; it defines the *types* of sources, leaving specific disciplines to later articles.
- It is the first substantive article of Book IV and is generally cited together with [[art1174]] (patrimonial character of the performance) and [[art1175]] (duty of good faith).
