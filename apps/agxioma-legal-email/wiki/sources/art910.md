# Art. 910 codice civile – Uso delle acque che limitano o attraversano un fondo

## Summary
Art. 910 of the Italian Civil Code (abrogated by D.P.R. 18 February 1999, n. 238) originally governed the right of a landowner to use non‑public water naturally flowing along or through his property for irrigation and industrial purposes, provided that drainage and surplus water were returned to the ordinary course. The norm was part of Book III (Property), Title II, Chapter II, Section IX (Waters). Today it has no legal effect.

## Key Details
- **Abrogation**: Repealed by art. 2 of D.P.R. 18 February 1999, n. 238.
- **Scope**: Applicable only to non‑public waters (acque non pubbliche) that naturally limit or cross a landholding.
- **Entitlement**: The owner could use the water only while it was flowing past his land.
- **Obligation**: All colature (drainage water) and avanzi (surplus) had to be returned to the ordinary watercourse.
- **Original position**: Located in Libro III, Della proprietà, Titolo II, Della proprietà fondiaria, Capo II, Sezione IX, Delle acque.
- **Historical context**: The article was part of a broader regime on water rights that has been superseded by modern water legislation (e.g., R.D. 1775/1933, D.Lgs. 152/2006).
