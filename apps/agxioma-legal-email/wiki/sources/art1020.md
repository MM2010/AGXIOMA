# Art. 1020 codice civile - Requisizione o espropriazione

## Summary
This article, located in Book III (Property), Title V (Usufruct, Use, and Habitation), Chapter I (Usufruct), Section IV (Extinction and Modifications of Usufruct), governs the effect of requisition or expropriation for public interest on a usufruct. When the encumbered property is taken under [[art834-cp]] (expropriation for public interest), the usufruct is not extinguished but automatically transfers to the indemnity paid to the owner. This ensures the usufructuary's right continues over the monetary compensation instead of the original asset.

## Key Details
- Article: 1020 of the Italian Civil Code (Codice Civile)
- Location: Book III – Property → Title V – Usufruct, Use, and Habitation → Chapter I – Usufruct → Section IV – Extinction and Modifications of Usufruct
- Principle: Usufruct does not terminate upon requisition or expropriation for public interest; it shifts to the indemnity.
- Reference: Cross-references [[art834-cp]] which defines expropriation for public interest.
- Effect: The usufructuary becomes entitled to the same rights over the indemnity (e.g., to receive its income or use proceeds) as they had over the original property.
- Scope: Applies both to requisition (temporary taking) and expropriation (permanent taking) when justified by public interest.
- Rationale: Preserves the economic substance of the usufruct despite the change in the encumbered asset.
