# Art. 952 Codice Civile - Costituzione del diritto di superficie

## Summary
Art. 952 of the Italian Civil Code regulates the establishment of the *diritto di superficie* (surface right). It empowers a landowner to grant another party the right to build and maintain a construction above the ground, with the grantee acquiring separate ownership of that construction. Alternatively, the owner may alienate the ownership of an already existing building separately from the land. This article is key to legally separating building ownership from land ownership, subject to formal requirements and registration.

## Key Details
- The surface right can be created in two ways: (1) granting a right to build and maintain a future construction; (2) transferring ownership of an already existing building while retaining land ownership.
- The right must be established by a public deed or private writing (art. 1350 n. 2 c.c.) and is subject to transcription in the land registry (art. 2643 n. 2 c.c.).
- The building remains owned separately from the land, overcoming the ordinary rule of accession (art. 934 c.c.) whereby anything built on the land belongs to the landowner.
- The surface right can be perpetual or for a fixed term; if no term is set, the right is considered perpetual (art. 956 c.c.).
- The right is a real right (*diritto reale*) and can be transferred, mortgaged (art. 2810 n. 3 c.c.), or inherited.
- Upon expiry of the term, the landowner reacquires ownership of the building by accession (art. 956 c.c.).
