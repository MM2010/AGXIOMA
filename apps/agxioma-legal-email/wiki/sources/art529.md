# Art. 529 codice penale – Atti e oggetti osceni: nozione

## Summary
Art. 529 of the Italian Penal Code defines what is considered "obscene" for criminal law purposes: acts and objects that, according to common sentiment, offend modesty (pudore). It establishes a key exception for works of art and science, which are not considered obscene unless sold or otherwise procured to a person under 18 for reasons other than study. This article underpins the entire system of offences against modesty and sexual honour under Title IX, Capo II.

## Key Details
- **Definition**: An act or object is obscene if it offends the common sense of modesty (pudore). The assessment is objective, based on the average community standard, not on individual sensibility.
- **Exception for art and science**: Works of art or science are excluded from the definition of obscenity, unless they are offered for sale, sold, or procured to a minor under 18 for purposes other than study.
- **Scope**: The definition applies throughout the Penal Code for any offence concerning obscene acts or objects (e.g., public obscene acts, distribution of obscene material).
- **Protection of minors**: The exception for art/science is lifted when the work is commercially directed at or provided to minors, reflecting a policy to shield them from explicit content.
- **Not a self-standing offence**: Art. 529 provides the definitional basis; it is not itself a punishable offence. It must be read together with articles that criminalise obscene conduct (e.g., [[art527-cp]], [[art528-cp]]).
