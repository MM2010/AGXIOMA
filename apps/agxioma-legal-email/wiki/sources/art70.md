# Art. 70 codice penale - Circostanze oggettive e soggettive

## Summary
Art. 70 c.p. provides the legal classification of circumstances of the offense (*circostanze del reato*) into objective and subjective categories, a distinction that governs the application of aggravating and mitigating factors under Italian criminal law. Objective circumstances concern the action’s modality, the harm or danger, or the victim’s personal conditions; subjective circumstances concern the intensity of intent or degree of fault, the offender’s personal conditions, or the relationship between offender and victim. It also specifies that circumstances inherent to the person of the offender relate to imputability and recidivism.

## Key Details
- **Objective circumstances** (art. 70, n. 1): relate to nature, species, means, object, time, place, and other modalities of the action; the gravity of damage or danger; or the conditions or personal qualities of the victim.
- **Subjective circumstances** (art. 70, n. 2): relate to the intensity of *dolo* (intent) or the degree of *colpa* (negligence); the conditions or personal qualities of the offender; the relationship between offender and victim; or matters inherent to the offender’s person.
- **Inherent to the person** (ultimo comma): specifically includes imputability (capacity to be held criminally responsible) and recidivism ([[art99-cp]]).
- The distinction affects how circumstances are evaluated, especially in balancing aggravating and mitigating factors under [[art69-cp]].
- The article is located in Book I, Title III, Chapter II of the Penal Code (“Delle circostanze del reato”).
- No explicit list of circumstances is exhaustive; the categories guide judicial interpretation.
