# Art. 801 Codice Civile - Revocation for Ingratitude

## Summary
Article 801 of the Italian Civil Code specifies the limited grounds on which a donor may seek revocation of a donation due to the donee’s ingratitude. The action is only permissible if the donee has committed one of the acts rendering them unworthy to inherit (as listed in Article 463, numbers 1–3), or has been guilty of serious injury toward the donor, has deliberately caused serious harm to the donor's property, or has wrongfully refused to provide legally owed alimony. This provision restricts the broad concept of ingratitude to clearly defined, serious misconduct, balancing the donor’s right to retract with the stability of completed gifts.

## Key Details
- Revocation for ingratitude under [[art800-cp|Art. 800]] can only be pursued if one of the conditions in Art. 801 is met.
- The donee must have committed one of the acts listed in Article 463, numbers 1, 2, or 3 (e.g., wilful homicide of the donor or certain ascendants/descendants, or attempt thereof).
- Alternatively, the donee may have been convicted of a serious injury (ingiuria grave) toward the donor.
- Deliberately causing serious economic harm to the donor’s estate also qualifies.
- Wrongful refusal to provide alimony when legally obligated under Articles 433, 435, and 436 triggers revocation.
- The action is subject to time limits under [[art802-cp|Art. 802]] and certain donations (e.g., remuneratory) are not revocable for ingratitude per [[art806-cp|Art. 806]].
- This article is contained in Book II (Successions), Title V (Donations), Chapter IV (Revocation of Donations) of the Codice Civile.
