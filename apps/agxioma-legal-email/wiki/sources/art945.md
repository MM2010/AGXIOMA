# Art. 945 codice civile - Isole e unioni di terra

## Summary
Art. 945 c.c. establishes the general rule that islands and unions of land formed within the bed of rivers or streams belong to the public domain. However, it provides two exceptions: (1) if the island was formed by avulsion (distacco improvviso e riconoscibile), the original owner of the land from which the detachment occurred retains ownership; (2) if a river or stream changes its course and surrounds a neighbouring owner's land (or part of it), turning it into an island, that owner also retains ownership.

## Key Details
- **Principle**: Islands and unions of land in riverbeds are public property (demanio pubblico).
- **Exception 1 – Avulsione**: If the island arises from avulsion ([[art944-cp]]), the owner of the land from which the soil was detached keeps ownership.
- **Exception 2 – New course forming an island**: If a river or stream diverts its course and surrounds a neighbouring owner's land (or part of it), that owner retains ownership of the island thus created.
- **Relation to alluvione/avulsione**: The article complements the rules on alluvione ([[art942-cp]]) and avulsione ([[art944-cp]]), distinguishing added land from islands.
- **Public domain status**: The default attribution to the State reflects the principle that riverbeds are public goods ([[art822-cp]]).
- **Interpretation**: The exceptions protect pre-existing private property rights against the natural formation of new land.
