# Art. 781 codice civile - Donazione tra coniugi

## Summary
Art. 781 of the Italian Civil Code originally prohibited any liberalities (donations) between spouses during the marriage, with the sole exception of gifts conforming to social usage (e.g., customary presents). This prohibition was intended to protect the freedom of the spouse and prevent undue influence. The article was abrogated by Law 151/1975 (family law reform), which eliminated the ban and brought donations between spouses under the ordinary rules of the donation contract. The provision is now of historical relevance and may still affect the validity of older donations made before 1975.

## Key Details
- Located in Book II (Successions), Title V (Donations), Chapter II (Capacity to dispose and receive by donation).
- Prohibition: spouses could not make any liberalities to each other during marriage.
- Exception: gifts "conformi agli usi" (in line with social customs, e.g., birthday or anniversary gifts of moderate value).
- The rule applied regardless of the value or form of the donation.
- Abrogated by art. 224 of Law 19 May 1975 n. 151 (riforma del diritto di famiglia).
- After abrogation, donations between spouses are valid provided they meet general requirements: public deed under penalty of nullity (art. 782 c.c.) and acceptance.
- The old prohibition could still be invoked for donations made while the article was in force, but such cases are now rare.
- The article reflects the former legal view that marriage could impair the freedom of the donor spouse.
