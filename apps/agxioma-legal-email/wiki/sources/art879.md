# Art. 879 codice civile - Edifici non soggetti all'obbligo delle distanze o a comunione forzosa

## Summary
Art. 879 c.c. establishes an exception to the general regime of legal distances and forced common ownership (comunione forzosa) of party walls under Sezione VI, Capo II, Titolo II of the Italian Civil Code. It exempts buildings belonging to the public domain (demanio pubblico) and those subject to the same regime (ex art. 824 c.c.), as well as buildings recognized as of historical, archaeological or artistic interest under relevant laws. The article also prevents a neighbor from exercising the faculty granted by Art. 877 (acquiring co-ownership of a wall built on the boundary). Moreover, constructions abutting public squares or roads are not subject to the ordinary distance rules but must comply with special laws and regulations.

## Key Details
- Excludes public domain buildings and those under the same regime (e.g., state property, assets of public entities) from forced common ownership of party walls.
- Excludes buildings of recognized historical, archaeological, or artistic interest (e.g., cultural heritage assets protected under D.Lgs. 42/2004).
- The neighbor cannot use the faculty of Art. 877 c.c. (to become co-owner of a wall built on the boundary) with respect to exempted buildings.
- Constructions on the boundary of public squares or roads are not subject to the general distance rules (e.g., Art. 873 c.c.), but must follow specific laws and regulations (e.g., highway codes, municipal building regulations).
- The norm reinforces the special status of certain buildings and public spaces, prioritizing public interest or cultural preservation over private property rights.
