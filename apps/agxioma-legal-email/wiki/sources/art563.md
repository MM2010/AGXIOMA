# Art. 563 codice penale - Estinzione del reato

## Summary
Art. 563 CP, part of the original Book II, Title XI (crimes against the family), Chapter I (crimes against marriage), provided that the crime of adultery ([[art559-cp]]) and concubinage ([[art560-cp]]) could be extinguished by remission of the complaint (even after conviction), the death of the offended spouse, or annulment of the offender's marriage. This extinction also extended to co-offenders and terminated the enforcement and penal effects of any conviction. The article was declared unconstitutional by the Italian Constitutional Court with decision 3 December 1969, n. 147, because the differential treatment of adulterous conduct violated the equality principle. Consequently, the original provisions for adultery and concubinage were also struck down, making the extinction rules moot.

## Key Details
- Declared unconstitutional by Corte Costituzionale, sent. 3 dicembre 1969, n. 147.
- Original scope: cases under artt. 559 (adultery) and 560 (concubinage) of the Penal Code.
- Extinction grounds: (1) remission of querela (even post-conviction); (2) death of the offended spouse; (3) annulment of the offender's marriage.
- Effect extended to co-offenders and participants; if a conviction existed, execution and penal effects ceased.
- The unconstitutional declaration was based on violation of Article 3 (equality) and Article 29 (family protection) of the Constitution, as the law penalised only the wife's adultery but not the husband's (except in concubinage).
- The article is therefore fully abrogated and no longer in force.
