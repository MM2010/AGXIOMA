# Art. 644 codice penale - Usura

## Summary
Article 644 of the Italian Penal Code criminalises usury – the act of obtaining or promising, for oneself or others, usurious interest or other advantages in exchange for a loan of money or other utility, except when the conduct falls under the crime of “circonvenzione di incapaci” (art. 643 cp). The offence also punishes intermediaries who procure loans for a usurious fee. The law establishes an absolute interest rate threshold beyond which interest is always deemed usurious, and also considers interest usurious if it is disproportionate to the loan given the debtor’s economic hardship. Penalties range from two to ten years of imprisonment and a fine between €5,000 and €30,000, with aggravating circumstances for professionals, banks, and when the victim is in need or is an entrepreneur.

## Key Details
- **Core conduct**: Making someone give or promise usurious interest or other advantages (in any form) as consideration for a loan of money or other utility (first paragraph).
- **Intermediary liability**: Procuring a loan and receiving a usurious commission, even without being an accomplice in the principal offence (second paragraph).
- **Absolute usury threshold**: Set by law (based on the average global effective rate – TEGM); interest exceeding that limit is always usurious (third paragraph).
- **Relative usury**: Interest below the threshold is still usurious if it is disproportionate considering the concrete modalities and the debtor’s economic or financial difficulty (fourth paragraph).
- **Calculation basis**: For determining the usurious rate, all commissions, fees of any kind, and expenses (except taxes and duties) connected to the credit must be included (fifth paragraph).
- **Base penalty**: Imprisonment from 2 to 10 years and a fine from €5,000 to €30,000.
- **Aggravating circumstances** (penalty increased by 1/3 to 1/2):
  - Offender acted in the exercise of a professional, banking, or financial intermediation activity.
  - Offender required shares, quotas, real estate, or business assets as guarantees.
  - Offender committed the offence against a person in need.
  - Offender committed the offence against an entrepreneur, professional, or artisan.
  - Offender is subject to a definitive special surveillance prevention measure (or up to 3 years after its end).
- **Confiscation**: Upon conviction (including plea bargain), confiscation is mandatory of the price or profit of the crime, or of assets up to the value of the usurious interest/advantages, without prejudice to the victim’s rights to restitution and damages (last paragraph).
