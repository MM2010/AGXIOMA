# Art. 49 codice penale - Reato supposto erroneamente e reato impossibile

## Summary
Art. 49 of the Italian Penal Code governs two distinct situations that exclude punishment: *reato supposto erroneamente* (putative crime) and *reato impossibile* (impossible crime). The first occurs when a person mistakenly believes they are committing a crime when the act is actually lawful; the second arises when the action is inherently incapable of causing the harmful or dangerous event due to the ineffectiveness of the act or the non‑existence of its object. In both cases, if the conduct nevertheless constitutes a different crime, the penalty for that crime applies. In the case of impossible crime, the judge may order a security measure even after acquittal.

## Key Details
- **Reato putativo:** Not punishable if a person commits an act that is not a crime, under the erroneous belief that it constitutes a crime.
- **Reato impossibile:** Punishment is excluded when, due to the unsuitability of the action or the non‑existence of its object, the harmful or dangerous event is impossible.
- **Conversion clause:** If the act contains the elements of a different crime, the penalty for that crime is applied (e.g., if the attempted crime is impossible but the conduct amounts to a preparatory act of another offence).
- **Security measure:** In the case of impossible crime, the judge may order that the acquitted defendant be subjected to a security measure (e.g., probation or confinement for dangerous persons).
- **Source reference:** Codice Penale, Libro I, Titolo III, Capo I (Art. 49).
